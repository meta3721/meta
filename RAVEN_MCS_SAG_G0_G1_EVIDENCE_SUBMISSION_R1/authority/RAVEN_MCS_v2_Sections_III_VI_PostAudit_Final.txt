\section{System Model and Support-Safe Target Alignment}
\label{sec:system_model}

This section defines the mobile selection process, the public deployment
target, and the finite-support difficulty that motivates RAVEN-MCS.  The key
distinction is between \emph{statistical target correction} and
\emph{support-safe correction}: even when an inverse-selection ratio has the
correct population interpretation, applying it in a finite mobile window can
change the effective client support and thereby alter the feasible
aggregation problem.  Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} therefore decides
whether the opportunity/Design correction may be enabled before the current
selection process is realized.

\subsection{Windowed-Asynchronous Mobile Crowdsensing}
\label{subsec:windowed_mcs}

We consider a mobile crowdsensing (MCS) system with a server and a set of
clients
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K=\{1,\ldots,K\}.
    \label{eq:client_set}
\end{equation}
The common prediction domain is partitioned into atomic units
$i\in\mathcal I$; an atomic unit may be written as $i=(n,t)$, where $n$
denotes a spatial unit and $t$ an absolute time index.  Unit $i$ has a
deployment target $Y_i$ and public deployment context
$C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}$.  All clients contribute to the common predictor
\begin{equation}
    \widehat Y_i
    =
    f_{\boldsymbol\theta}
    \!\left(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\right).
    \label{eq:common_predictor_v2}
\end{equation}
When client $k$ obtains a sensing value for unit $i$ in server window $r$, we
denote it by $Z_{k,r,i}$.

Server time is divided into aggregation windows
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal W_r=[T_r,T_{r+1}),
    \qquad r=0,1,\ldots .
    \label{eq:window_v2}
\end{equation}
The server keeps $\boldsymbol\theta_r$ fixed during $\mathcal W_r$.  Client
$k$ may start local training from a previously downloaded model
$\boldsymbol\theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}$, $\sigma_{k,r}\le r$, with model-version
staleness
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{k,r}
    \triangleq
    r-\sigma_{k,r}.
    \label{eq:staleness_v2}
\end{equation}
Updates may arrive at different times inside a window, but an individual
arrival does not immediately modify the global model.  The server first
closes the window, determines which registered attempts are usable, computes
one aggregation vector, and then performs the single transition
$\boldsymbol\theta_r\!\rightarrow\!\boldsymbol\theta_{r+1}$.  We therefore
call the system \emph{windowed-asynchronous}; it is distinct from
immediate-update FedAsync.

\subsection{Sequential Mobile Selection and Legal Information}
\label{subsec:selection_process_v2}

The two-stage MCS process contains three selection layers.  The sensing-side
stage consists of mobile opportunity and actual observation, whereas the
update-side stage determines whether a registered client attempt becomes
usable before window closure.

For client $k$, window $r$, and atomic unit $i$, let
\begin{equation}
    R_{k,r,i}\in\{0,1\}
    \label{eq:R_indicator_v2}
\end{equation}
denote whether $(k,i)$ belongs to the mobile opportunity/risk set before the
current sensing outcome is revealed.  Conditional on $R_{k,r,i}=1$,
\begin{equation}
    O_{k,r,i}\in\{0,1\}
    \label{eq:O_indicator_v2}
\end{equation}
indicates whether unit $i$ is actually observed.  A client with a nonempty
observed buffer registers an update attempt; the registered-client set is
$\mathcal E_r$.  For $k\in\mathcal E_r$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 U_{k,r}
 =
 \mathbf 1\{&
 \text{computation and transmission succeed,}\\
 &\text{arrival}\le T_{r+1},
 \ \tau_{k,r}\le S_{\max}\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:U_indicator_v2}
\end{equation}
and the usable set
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal U_r
    =
    \{k\in\mathcal E_r:U_{k,r}=1\}.
    \label{eq:usable_set_v2}
\end{equation}
The final aggregation set is a subset of $\mathcal U_r$ after reconstruction
removes clients with zero effective mass.  Failed computation, transmission,
deadline, or staleness events remain explicitly recorded as $U_{k,r}=0$.

For theoretical bookkeeping, let $\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$ denote the
set (or multiset) of realized opportunity events in window $r$.  A generic
opportunity event $e\in\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$ carries the mark
$X_{r,e}=(k_e,i_e)$.  With $\mathcal F_{r^-}$ denoting the pre-window
information set defined below, its conditional mark law is
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\!\left(
   X_{r,e}=(k,i)
   \mid
   \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right)
 =
 P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i).
 \label{eq:opp_event_mark_law_v2}
\end{equation}
where the normalized opportunity-event law is
\begin{equation}
 P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)
 =
 \pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i),r}
 \nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s(i)),
 \qquad
 \sum_{k,i}P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)=1.
 \label{eq:opp_event_law_v2}
\end{equation}
Here $\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}$ is the client--stratum opportunity mass
and $\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s)$ is the conditional atomic
opportunity law.  The realized indicators $R_{k,r,i}$ remain the
implementation-level risk-set bookkeeping induced by the same mobile
exposure process; they are not assumed to be independent Bernoulli draws and
are not identified with the normalized event-mark probabilities in
\eqref{eq:opp_event_law_v2}.

The subsequent observation and usable-update probabilities are
\begin{align}
 p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
 &\triangleq
 \Pr\!\left(
 O_{k,r,i}=1
 \mid
 R_{k,r,i}=1,
 \mathcal H^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
 \right),
 \label{eq:pobs_def_v2}\\
 q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}
 &\triangleq
 \Pr\!\left(
 U_{k,r}=1
 \mid
 k\in\mathcal E_r,
 \mathcal H^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}
 \right).
 \label{eq:quse_def_v2}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ contains only information measurable
before $O_{k,r,i}$ is realized.  The usable-update history
$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ is measured after sensing-side registration
but before $U_{k,r}$ is realized; it may contain the registered attempt state,
but not the realized computation/transmission outcome, future events, or the
current random update used for aggregation.

The support-aware Gate introduced in Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} obeys a
stricter pre-window boundary.  Let $\mathcal F_{r^-}$ denote the
$\sigma$-field available immediately before the current opportunity process
$R_r$ is realized.  It contains the public target, frozen hyperparameters,
completed-window selection metadata, lagged opportunity/propensity states,
completed-window support audits, and any exogenous context measured before
the current opportunity process.  It excludes the current/future information set
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal I_r^{\mathrm{excl}}
 ={}&\{R_r,O_r,U_r,Z_r\}\\
 &\cup\{\text{current loss or update information}\}\\
 &\cup\{\text{future or test outcomes}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gate_information_exclusion}
\end{equation}
The Gate variable $G_r$ must be $\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable and is frozen
for the entire window.  This predictability requirement prevents the current
selection realization from retrospectively deciding whether that same
realization should receive the opportunity/Design correction.

\subsection{Deployment Groups, Opportunity Strata, and Public Target}
\label{subsec:target_measures_v2}

We distinguish two structural mappings.  The \emph{deployment group}
$h(i)\in\mathcal G$ identifies the application-level mass the deployed
predictor should serve, whereas the \emph{opportunity stratum}
$s(i)\in\mathcal S$ pools repeated mobile exposure patterns for selection
modeling.  They are different scientific objects:
\begin{equation}
    h(i)\neq s(i)
    \quad\text{in general}.
    \label{eq:h_neq_s_v2}
\end{equation}
Both mappings are frozen from public/training-side information and never use
test-label values.

We begin from a public, label-independent atomic deployment law
\begin{equation}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}\ge0,
    \qquad
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{dep}}}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}
    =1.
    \label{eq:atomic_target_v2}
\end{equation}
Its deployment-group marginal is
\begin{equation}
    \mu_g
    =
    \sum_{i:h(i)=g}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}},
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol\mu\in\Delta^{|\mathcal G|-1}.
    \label{eq:target_group_mass_v2}
\end{equation}

For selection reconstruction, define the target mass of opportunity stratum
$s$ as
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}
    =
    \sum_{i:s(i)=s}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}.
    \label{eq:target_stratum_mass_v2}
\end{equation}
For $\Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}>0$,
\begin{equation}
    \nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s}
    =
    \frac{\varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}}
         {\Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}},
    \qquad s(i)=s,
    \label{eq:target_conditional_atomic_v2}
\end{equation}
and let $\lambda^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k\mid s}$ be a frozen, public,
support-compatible client share satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \lambda^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k\mid s}\ge0,
    \qquad
    \sum_k\lambda^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k\mid s}=1.
    \label{eq:target_client_share_v2}
\end{equation}
We then define
\begin{equation}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}
 \lambda^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k\mid s},
 &\Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}>0,\\
 0,&\Lambda_s^{\mathrm{tar}}=0,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:target_ks_mass_v2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s(i)}
 \nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s(i)},
 &\Lambda_{s(i)}^{\mathrm{tar}}>0,\\
 0,&\Lambda_{s(i)}^{\mathrm{tar}}=0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:target_ki_mass_v2}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
    \sum_k\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
    =
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}},
    \qquad
    \sum_{k,s}\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}=1.
    \label{eq:target_compatibility_v2}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:target_compatibility_v2} is the compatibility bridge between
the application-level deployment target and the client--stratum target used
for opportunity correction.

At the operational client--stratum granularity, define the target support
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal S^{\mathrm{tar}}
    =
    \{(k,s):\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}>0\}.
    \label{eq:target_support_v2}
\end{equation}
The opportunity support in window $r$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal S^{\mathrm{opp}}_r
    =
    \{(k,s):\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}>0\}.
    \label{eq:opp_support_v2}
\end{equation}
These sets need not coincide.  Missing opportunity support on
$\mathcal S^{\mathrm{tar}}$ creates a positivity problem, whereas substantial
opportunity mass outside $\mathcal S^{\mathrm{tar}}$ can be suppressed by
Full Design and thereby change the finite-window effective client support.
This asymmetry is the key support-safety issue addressed in Section~IV.

\subsection{Deployment-Target Risk and Arrival Risk}
\label{subsec:target_risk_v2}

For a loss $\ell(\widehat Y,Y)$, the deployment-target risk is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\mu}(\boldsymbol\theta)
 =
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{dep}}}
 \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}
 \ell\!\left(
 f_{\boldsymbol\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}),
 Y_i
 \right).
 \label{eq:target_risk_v2}
\end{equation}
By contrast, the data reaching learning are distributed according to
opportunity, observation, and usable-update selection.  For evaluation,
quantities without a window index denote frozen evaluation-period marginals
of their corresponding legal pre-outcome mechanisms.  A label-free atomic
arrival intensity is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 =
 \sum_k
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i)}
 \widehat\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k}(i\mid s(i))
 \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,i}
 \widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,s(i)}.
 \label{eq:arrival_intensity_v2}
\end{equation}
Normalize
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 =
 \frac{
   \widehat r_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 }{
   \sum_{j\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{dep}}}
   \widehat r_j^{\mathrm{arr}}
 },
 \label{eq:arrival_distribution_v2}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\rho}(\boldsymbol\theta)
 =
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{dep}}}
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 \ell\!\left(
 f_{\boldsymbol\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}),
 Y_i
 \right).
 \label{eq:arrival_risk_v2}
\end{equation}
The two risks answer different questions.  In general,
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathrm{arr}}\neq
\boldsymbol\varpi^{\mathrm{tar}}$, so optimizing the distribution induced by
successfully delivered updates need not optimize the deployment population
of interest.  Proposition~1 in Section~VI formalizes this distinction.

\subsection{Finite-Support Incompatibility and the Design Objective}
\label{subsec:support_safe_problem}

The previous subsection motivates target correction statistically, but does
not establish that a full correction is safe in a finite mobile window.
Full opportunity correction and its No-Design fallback can assign different
record masses to the same realized selection trace.  These differences can
alter the within-client composition, effective sample size, variance proxy,
and, in extreme cases, whether a client retains any target-relevant mass at
all.  Hence a statistically meaningful target ratio can modify the support
and geometry of the subsequent feasible aggregation problem.

To make this distinction explicit, we compare two same-window
counterfactual reconstruction modes under the same exogenous selection trace:
\begin{align}
    D &: \text{Full opportunity/Design correction}, \nonumber\\
    0 &: \text{No-Design RAVEN fallback}.
    \label{eq:counterfactual_modes}
\end{align}
Mode $0$ removes only the opportunity/Design ratio; observation correction,
usable-update correction, feasible calibration, and the remaining RAVEN
pipeline are unchanged.  The comparison is therefore a support-safety
comparison, not a comparison against an uncorrected FL method.

Section~IV constructs, for each window, corrected within-client deployment
compositions
$\mathbf c^g_{k,r}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal G|-1}$ and opportunity-stratum
compositions
$\mathbf c^s_{k,r}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal S|-1}$.  For the realized final
aggregation set $\mathcal A_r$, let
\begin{equation}
    M_r
    =
    [\mathbf c^g_{k,r}]_{k\in\mathcal A_r}.
    \label{eq:Mr_def_v2}
\end{equation}
Section~V defines the feasible aggregation set $\mathcal C_r$.  Its image in
deployment-group space is
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_r
    \triangleq
    \{
      M_r\boldsymbol\alpha:
      \boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathcal C_r
    \}.
    \label{eq:feasible_target_image_v2}
\end{equation}
The instantaneous irreducible feasibility gap is
\begin{equation}
    d_r^{\mathrm{feas}}
    \triangleq
    \min_{\boldsymbol\omega\in\Omega_r}
    \|
      \boldsymbol\omega-\boldsymbol\mu
    \|_2.
    \label{eq:feasibility_gap_v2}
\end{equation}
Thus $d_r^{\mathrm{feas}}>0$ means that no aggregation rule using the same
finite-window support and constraints can exactly realize the public target
in that window.  RAVEN-MCS therefore does not ask the reconstruction stage to
synthesize unavailable support.

For an active window, the effective deployment-group composition is
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\omega_r
    =
    M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r.
    \label{eq:omega_r_v2}
\end{equation}
Let $\eta_r>0$ be the server step size,
$I_r=\mathbf 1\{|\mathcal A_r|>0\}$, and
\begin{equation}
    S_R
    =
    \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
    \eta_r I_r.
    \label{eq:active_mass_v2}
\end{equation}
For $S_R>0$, define
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R
    =
    \frac{1}{S_R}
    \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
    \eta_r I_r
    \boldsymbol\omega_r,
    \label{eq:omega_bar_v2}
\end{equation}
and the deployment-group structural mismatch
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{group}}(R)
    =
    \|
      \overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R
      -
      \boldsymbol\mu
    \|_1.
    \label{eq:delta_group_v2}
\end{equation}

For client--stratum structure, extend $\alpha_{k,r}=0$ to
$k\notin\mathcal A_r$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)
 =
 \frac{1}{S_R}
 \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
 \eta_r I_r
 \alpha_{k,r}
 c^s_{k,r,s},
 \label{eq:Pi_hat_ks_v2}
\end{equation}
so that
$\sum_{k,s}\widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)=1$.  The finer structural mismatch is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{c\text{-}s}(R)
 =
 \sum_{k,s}
 \left|
   \widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)
   -
   \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 \right|.
 \label{eq:delta_cs_v2}
\end{equation}

The resulting design problem has three layers.  First, the predictor should
be judged under the public deployment risk $\mathcal R_\mu$, not under the
arrival risk by convention.  Second, opportunity correction should be enabled
only when its finite-support perturbation relative to the No-Design RAVEN
fallback is predictably controlled.  Third, the corrected reference must be
mapped to the realizable set $\Omega_r$ rather than to an unavailable target
composition.  We therefore seek
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{low deployment-target prediction risk},\\
&\text{low }
  \Delta_{\mathrm{group}}(R)
  \text{ and }
  \Delta_{c\text{-}s}(R),\\
&\text{and support-safe use of Full Design under }
  \mathcal F_{r^-}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:support_safe_goal}
\end{equation}
The support-aware Gate that operationalizes the third requirement is developed
in Section~IV.  Importantly, prediction no-harm is not imposed through
current/test outcomes in the online Gate; it is a frozen validation/test
criterion in Section~VII.  Section~V then performs feasibility-aware
calibration within the finite-window image $\Omega_r$, and Section~VI states
the corresponding identification and structural-safety guarantees.

\section{Support-Aware Sequential Target Reconstruction}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

Section~\ref{sec:system_model} separates the public deployment target from the
distribution induced by mobile opportunity, observation, and usable-update
selection.  A conventional response would always apply the corresponding
inverse-selection correction.  This is insufficient in a finite mobile
window: Full Design can suppress available mass outside the target support,
concentrate record weights, alter the set of clients with positive corrected
mass, and thereby change the feasible calibration problem itself.  RAVEN-MCS
therefore makes the opportunity correction \emph{conditional on a predictable
support-safety certificate}.  The Gate is frozen before the current
opportunity process is realized; observation and usable-update corrections
then retain their original stage-specific legal-information boundaries.

\subsection{Full-Design and No-Design Reconstruction Modes}
\label{subsec:design_modes}

For an atomic client--unit mark with positive opportunity probability, the
ideal opportunity/Design ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}
 =
 \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s(i)}}
      {\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i),r}}
 \frac{\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s(i)}}
      {\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s(i))}
 =
 \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}}
      {P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)}.
 \label{eq:design_ratio_general_v2}
\end{equation}
Under the within-stratum exchangeability used by the main implementation,
$\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s)=\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s}$, so the
operational ratio reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,r,s}
 =
 \frac{
   \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 }{
   \max\{
     \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r},
     \pi_{\min}
   \}
 }.
 \label{eq:design_ratio_full_v2}
\end{equation}
For atomic bookkeeping, the operational stratum ratio is lifted by
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,r,i}
 \triangleq
 \widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,r,s(i)},
 \qquad
 \widehat\zeta^{o,0}_{k,r,i}
 \triangleq 1.
 \label{eq:design_ratio_atomic_lift_v2}
\end{equation}
The superscript $D$ denotes \emph{Full Design}.  Its same-pipeline
No-Design fallback is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat\zeta^{o,0}_{k,r,s}=1.
    \label{eq:design_ratio_nod_v2}
\end{equation}
Only the opportunity/Design ratio changes between these two modes;
observation correction, usable-update correction, feasible calibration, and
the global update remain common.

Opportunity estimation is lagged.  One admissible implementation maintains
\begin{align}
 C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
 &=
 \rho_{\mathrm{opp}}
 C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r-1}
 +
 N^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r-1},
 \label{eq:opp_ema_v2}\\
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
 &=
 \frac{
   C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
   +
   \epsilon_{\mathrm{opp}}
 }{
   \sum_{j,u}
   \left(
     C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{j,u,r}
     +
     \epsilon_{\mathrm{opp}}
   \right)
 }.
 \label{eq:opp_estimate_v2}
\end{align}
Other estimators are admissible provided that they are measurable with
respect to the pre-window information set of Section~III.  Importantly, the
smoothed point estimate in \eqref{eq:opp_estimate_v2} is not itself treated as
a positivity certificate.

\subsection{Predictable Support-Safety Certificates}
\label{subsec:sag_certificates}

Before the current $R/O/U$ realization, SAG summarizes two distinct kinds of
evidence.  The first consists of interpretable support and weight-stability
diagnostics; the second directly bounds the probability and magnitude of the
Full-Design perturbation relative to the No-Design fallback.  All online
certificates below are $\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable.

\subsubsection{Target-support coverage and retained opportunity mass}

For each target-positive client--stratum pair, let
$\underline\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}$ be a one-sided lower safety bound on the
current opportunity mass.  The certified target-support coverage is
\begin{equation}
 \underline C^{\mathrm{cov}}_r
 =
 \sum_{k,s}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 \mathbf 1
 \left\{
   \underline\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
   \ge
   \pi_{\mathrm{safe}}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:sag_cov_v2}
\end{equation}
Thus a numerically positive smoothed estimate does not certify support unless
its lower bound exceeds the frozen safety floor $\pi_{\mathrm{safe}}$.

A different risk arises when substantial opportunity mass lies outside the
target-positive support and would therefore be attenuated or removed by Full
Design.  Let $\bar\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r^-}$ be the predictable lagged
opportunity law.  Its retained mass inside the target support is
\begin{equation}
 C^{\mathrm{ret}}_{r^-}
 =
 \sum_{k,s}
 \bar\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r^-}
 \mathbf 1
 \left\{
   \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}>0
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:sag_ret_point_v2}
\end{equation}
The Gate uses a conservative lower certificate
\begin{equation}
    \underline C^{\mathrm{ret}}_r
    \le
    C^{\mathrm{ret}}_{r},
    \label{eq:sag_ret_lcb_v2}
\end{equation}
whose construction is calibrated on completed development windows.

\subsubsection{ESS retention and Design-induced clipping}

For a completed window $t$, reconstruct Full-Design and No-Design first-stage
weights on the same selection trace.  For $m\in\{D,0\}$, let
\begin{equation}
 a^{m}_{k,t,i}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 a_{\max},
 \frac{
   \widehat\zeta^{o,m}_{k,t,i}
 }{
   \max\{
     \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,t,i},
     p_{\min}
   \}
 }
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:counterfactual_a_v2}
\end{equation}
The corresponding effective sample size is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{ESS}^{m}_{k,t}
 =
 \frac{
   \left(
     \sum_i
     R_{k,t,i}
     O_{k,t,i}
     a^{m}_{k,t,i}
   \right)^2
 }{
   \sum_i
   \left(
     R_{k,t,i}
     O_{k,t,i}
     a^{m}_{k,t,i}
   \right)^2
   +
   \varepsilon_0
 }.
 \label{eq:cf_ess_v2}
\end{equation}
For eligible clients define
\begin{equation}
 e_{k,t}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 1,
 \frac{
   \operatorname{ESS}^{D}_{k,t}
 }{
   \max\{
     \operatorname{ESS}^{0}_{k,t},
     1
   \}
 }
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:ess_ret_client_v2}
\end{equation}
Completed windows provide realized ESS-retention statistics; a frozen
calibration map converts their lagged history and other pre-window support
features into the lower certificate
$\underline C^{\mathrm{ESS}}_r$ used online.

To distinguish instability caused specifically by Design from clipping that
would already occur under observation correction, define the unclipped
candidates
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde a^{D}_{k,t,i}
 =
 \frac{
   \widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,t,i}
 }{
   \max\{
     \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,t,i},
     p_{\min}
   \}
 },
 \qquad
 \widetilde a^{0}_{k,t,i}
 =
 \frac{1}{
   \max\{
     \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,t,i},
     p_{\min}
   \}
 }.
 \label{eq:unclipped_candidates_v2}
\end{equation}
A clipping event is called Design-induced when
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde a^{D}_{k,t,i}>a_{\max}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \widetilde a^{0}_{k,t,i}\le a_{\max}.
 \label{eq:design_induced_clip_event_v2}
\end{equation}
The online Gate uses a predictable upper certificate
$\overline C^{\mathrm{clip}}_r$ for the corresponding near-term clipping
burden.  Observation-layer clipping common to both modes is not counted as
Design-induced clipping.

\subsubsection{Active-set risk and calibration perturbation}

The most consequential support failure is a change in the usable
positive-mass client set.  Let
$\mathcal A_r^D$ and $\mathcal A_r^0$ denote the counterfactual aggregation
sets that Full Design and No Design would produce on the same exogenous
selection trace.  Their conditional mismatch risk is
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{A,r}
 =
 \Pr\!\left(
   \mathcal A_r^D
   \neq
   \mathcal A_r^0
   \mid
   \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right).
 \label{eq:active_set_risk_v2}
\end{equation}
SAG uses a frozen, calibrated upper certificate
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{A,r}
    \le
    \overline\delta_{A,r}.
    \label{eq:active_set_ucb_v2}
\end{equation}

When the two modes share the same active set, they can still produce
different reference vectors, composition matrices, and variance states.  The
post-window audits therefore record
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\beta,t}
 &=
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\beta_t^D
   -
   \boldsymbol\beta_t^0
 \right\|_2,
 \label{eq:delta_beta_audit_v2}\\
 \Delta_{M,t}
 &=
 \left\|
   M_t^D
   -
   M_t^0
 \right\|_2,
 \label{eq:delta_M_audit_v2}\\
 \Delta_{V,t}
 &=
 \left\|
   V_t^D
   -
   V_t^0
 \right\|_2.
 \label{eq:delta_V_audit_v2}
\end{align}
From completed common-support windows, the certificate module produces
$\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable upper bounds
$\overline\Delta_{\beta,r}$,
$\overline\Delta_{M,r}$, and
$\overline\Delta_{V,r}$.  Let
\begin{equation}
    B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}
    =
    \sqrt{K}
    \label{eq:BM_safe_v2}
\end{equation}
be the universal predictable bound used by the main implementation; any
tighter replacement must itself be $\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable.  Let
$B_{Q,r}=\|\mathbf Q_r\|_2$, where $\mathbf Q_r$ is the optional
pre-window target-debt state of Section~V.  With the frozen calibration
coefficients of Section~V, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \overline D^{\alpha}_r
 &=
 \overline\Delta_{\beta,r}
 +
 \frac{
   B_{Q,r}
   +
   \lambda_g
   \left(
     2B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}+1
   \right)
 }{
   \lambda_{\beta}
 }
 \overline\Delta_{M,r}\\
 &\quad+
 \frac{\lambda_v}{\lambda_{\beta}}
 \overline\Delta_{V,r}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Dalpha_cert_v2}
\end{equation}
The corresponding common-support service-composition certificate is
\begin{equation}
 \overline B^{\mathrm{srv}}_r
 =
 \sqrt{|\mathcal G|}
 \left(
   \overline\Delta_{M,r}
   +
   B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}
   \overline D^{\alpha}_r
 \right).
 \label{eq:Bsrv_cert_v2}
\end{equation}
Finally, let $\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}$ be the frozen conditional failure level
of the common-support perturbation certificate.  The total structural-safety
certificate is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \overline B^{\mathrm{tot}}_r
 =
 \overline B^{\mathrm{srv}}_r
 +
 2\overline\delta_{A,r}
 +
 2\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}.
 }
 \label{eq:Btot_cert_v2}
\end{equation}
Section~VI proves the common-support sensitivity bound underlying
\eqref{eq:Dalpha_cert_v2}--\eqref{eq:Bsrv_cert_v2} and gives the precise
expected structural-harm interpretation of
$\overline B^{\mathrm{tot}}_r$.

\subsection{Support-Aware Gate}
\label{subsec:sag_gate}

Let the frozen threshold vector be
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\tau^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 =
 \left(
 \tau_{\mathrm{cov}},
 \tau_{\mathrm{ret}},
 \tau_{\mathrm{ESS}},
 \tau_{\mathrm{clip}},
 \tau_A,
 \tau_{\mathrm{safe}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:sag_threshold_vector_v2}
\end{equation}
The certificate failure level $\delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$ (or its frozen
pre-window rule) is fixed together with these thresholds, the look-back
horizon, the propensity models, and the certificate models before unseen
evaluation.

The binary Support-Aware Gate is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 G_r
 =
 \mathbf 1
 \left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 \underline C^{\mathrm{cov}}_r
 \ge\tau_{\mathrm{cov}},\\
 \underline C^{\mathrm{ret}}_r
 \ge\tau_{\mathrm{ret}},\\
 \underline C^{\mathrm{ESS}}_r
 \ge\tau_{\mathrm{ESS}},\\
 \overline C^{\mathrm{clip}}_r
 \le\tau_{\mathrm{clip}},\\
 \overline\delta_{A,r}
 \le\tau_A,\\
 \overline B^{\mathrm{tot}}_r
 \le\tau_{\mathrm{safe}}
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
}
\label{eq:sag_gate_v2}
\end{equation}
If any required certificate is missing, non-finite, outside its calibrated
applicability region, or unavailable during cold start, SAG fails closed:
\begin{equation}
    G_r=0.
    \label{eq:sag_fail_closed_v2}
\end{equation}
Crucially, $G_r$ is computed entirely from $\mathcal F_{r^-}$ and is frozen
before the current opportunity process is realized.  Dataset identity,
current $R/O/U$, sensing values, current prediction residuals, and test
outcomes are not Gate inputs.

The gated Design ratio used throughout window $r$ is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}
 =
 1
 +
 G_r
 \left(
   \widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,r,i}
   -
   1
 \right).
 \label{eq:gated_design_ratio_v2}
\end{equation}
Thus $G_r=1$ gives Full Design and $G_r=0$ gives the No-Design RAVEN
fallback without disabling the remaining correction and calibration
pipeline.

The thresholds are not optimized online.  Candidate grids and certificate
models are formed on development data, then a safe-first validation protocol
first enforces a predeclared prediction no-harm margin and structural no-harm
relative to the No-Design fallback, verifies certificate coverage, minimizes
false enables, and only then prefers larger target-alignment benefit.  All
choices are frozen before genuinely unseen environments are opened.
Section~VII specifies the experimental instantiation and statistical
protocol.

\subsection{Gated Observation Correction and Local H\'ajek Update}
\label{subsec:gated_hajek}

After $G_r$ has been frozen, the current opportunity process $R_r$ is
realized.  Before any current observation outcome is revealed, the server or
client evaluates and freezes
$\widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ from the legal
$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$.  Only then is the current observation
process $O_r$ realized.  The inverse factor applied after observing $O_r$
therefore uses a value fixed before that same outcome.  The ideal gated
opportunity ratio corresponding to a predictable scalar $g_r\in[0,1]$ is
\begin{equation}
 \zeta^{o,(g)}_{k,r,i}
 =
 1
 +
 g_r
 \left(
   \frac{
     \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
   }{
     P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)
   }
   -
   1
 \right).
 \label{eq:ideal_gated_ratio_v2}
\end{equation}
The operational binary SAG uses $g_r=G_r$ and the plug-in version
\eqref{eq:gated_design_ratio_v2}.  The stabilized first-stage record weight
is
\begin{equation}
 a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 a_{\max},
 \frac{
   \widetilde\zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}
 }{
   \max\{
     \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i},
     p_{\min}
   \}
 }
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:a_sag_v2}
\end{equation}
Define the corrected total mass
\begin{equation}
 m_{k,r}
 =
 \sum_i
 R_{k,r,i}
 O_{k,r,i}
 a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}},
 \label{eq:m_total_v2}
\end{equation}
the deployment-group mass
\begin{equation}
 m^g_{k,r,g}
 =
 \sum_i
 R_{k,r,i}
 O_{k,r,i}
 a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 \mathbf 1\{h(i)=g\},
 \label{eq:m_group_v2}
\end{equation}
and the opportunity-stratum mass
\begin{equation}
 m^s_{k,r,s}
 =
 \sum_i
 R_{k,r,i}
 O_{k,r,i}
 a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 \mathbf 1\{s(i)=s\}.
 \label{eq:m_stratum_v2}
\end{equation}
Before the usable-update event is realized, define the positive-mass
training/attempt set
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal B_r
    =
    \{k\in\mathcal E_r:m_{k,r}>0\}.
    \label{eq:preU_training_set_v2}
\end{equation}
Every $k\in\mathcal B_r$ has normalized record weights
\begin{equation}
 \overline a_{k,r,i}
 =
 \frac{
   R_{k,r,i}
   O_{k,r,i}
   a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 }{
   m_{k,r}
 },
 \label{eq:abar_v2}
\end{equation}
deployment-group composition
\begin{equation}
    c^g_{k,r,g}
    =
    \frac{m^g_{k,r,g}}{m_{k,r}},
    \label{eq:ckrg_v2}
\end{equation}
and opportunity-stratum composition
\begin{equation}
    c^s_{k,r,s}
    =
    \frac{m^s_{k,r,s}}{m_{k,r}}.
    \label{eq:ckrs_v2}
\end{equation}
Both composition vectors sum to one.

The local effective sample size is
\begin{equation}
 n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
   m_{k,r}^{2}
 }{
   \sum_i
   \left(
     R_{k,r,i}
     O_{k,r,i}
     a_{k,r,i}^{\mathrm{SAG}}
   \right)^2
 }
 =
 \frac{1}{
   \sum_i
   \overline a_{k,r,i}^{\,2}
 }.
 \label{eq:neff_v2}
\end{equation}
It measures weight concentration rather than target-equivalent mass.

Only after the Gate is frozen and the current observation process has
realized does the learning routine access the observed values.  Each $k\in\mathcal B_r$
minimizes the local H\'ajek-normalized completion objective
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F^H_{k,r}(\boldsymbol\theta)
 =
 \sum_i
 \overline a_{k,r,i}
 \frac{1}{2}
 \left[
 f_{\boldsymbol\theta}
 \!\left(
   i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}
 \right)
 -
 Z_{k,r,i}
 \right]^2.
 \label{eq:local_hajek_objective_v2}
\end{equation}
Starting from $\boldsymbol\theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}$, client $k$ performs
$E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ local steps with step size $\gamma_r$.  Its normalized
model displacement is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf u_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
   \boldsymbol\theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}
   -
   \boldsymbol\theta^{(E_{\mathrm{loc}})}_{k,r}
 }{
   \gamma_r E_{\mathrm{loc}}
 }.
 \label{eq:normalized_update_v2}
\end{equation}
Neither $G_r$ nor its thresholds can change after seeing
$Z_{k,r,i}$, the local loss, or $\mathbf u_{k,r}$.

\subsection{Usable-Update Correction and Reference Mass}
\label{subsec:usable_correction_v2}

The update-side correction remains distinct from the sensing-side Gate.
After local computation/transmission is initiated from $\mathcal B_r$, the
legal pre-$U$ history $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ is formed.  Before
$U_{k,r}$ is revealed, RAVEN evaluates and freezes
$\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ using only this pre-$U$ information.  The
usable-update event is then realized, and the actual aggregation set becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_r
 =
 \{k\in\mathcal B_r:U_{k,r}=1\}
 =
 \mathcal B_r\cap\mathcal U_r.
 \label{eq:aggregation_set_v2}
\end{equation}
The already computed masses, compositions, and effective sample sizes for
clients in $\mathcal B_r$ are simply restricted to $\mathcal A_r$ for server
calibration.  For $k\in\mathcal A_r$, define
\begin{equation}
 d_{k,r}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 d_{\max},
 \frac{1}{
   \max\{
     \widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r},
     q_{\min}
   \}
 }
 \right\},
 \label{eq:d_use_v2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    b_{k,r}
    =
    m_{k,r}d_{k,r}.
    \label{eq:b_mass_v2}
\end{equation}
The selection-corrected reference vector is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\beta_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
   b_{k,r}
 }{
   \sum_{j\in\mathcal A_r}
   b_{j,r}
 },
 \qquad
 k\in\mathcal A_r.
 \label{eq:beta_ref_v2}
\end{equation}
Hence
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ is a probability vector over the current
aggregation set.

For theory, define the ideal untruncated gated first-stage weight
\begin{equation}
 a^{\star,(g)}_{k,r,i}
 =
 \frac{
   \zeta^{o,(g)}_{k,r,i}
 }{
   p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
 }.
 \label{eq:a_star_gated_v2}
\end{equation}
Together with
$d^\star_{k,r}=1/q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$, a realized record with mark
$(k,i)$ receives the operational ideal factor
\begin{equation}
 O_{k,r,i}
 U_{k,r}
 \frac{
   \zeta^{o,(g)}_{k,r,i}
 }{
   p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
   q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}
 }.
 \label{eq:factorized_global_weight_gated_v2}
\end{equation}
For identification, this factor is integrated with respect to the normalized
opportunity-event mark law $P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$ of Section~III, rather than by
identifying $P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)$ with the expectation of the risk-set
indicator $R_{k,r,i}$.  Section~VI shows that the resulting ideal event-level
conditional corrected measure is exactly a predictable interpolation between
the deployment target and the opportunity law.  The
implemented clipped plug-in weights in \eqref{eq:a_sag_v2} and
\eqref{eq:d_use_v2} are finite-sample stabilizers and are not claimed to be
exactly unbiased when their bounds are active.

Before server calibration, the reference deployment composition is
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\omega_r^{\mathrm{ref}}
    =
    M_r
    \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r.
    \label{eq:omega_ref_v2}
\end{equation}
This vector need not equal $\boldsymbol\mu$: even a statistically coherent
reference can lie outside the finite-window feasible target image
$\Omega_r$ defined in Section~III.  Section~V therefore treats
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ as a reference rather than an automatically
feasible final aggregation decision.

\subsection{Post-Window Counterfactual Audit}
\label{subsec:post_window_audit}

After window $r$ closes, RAVEN reconstructs both Full-Design and No-Design
counterfactual states on the \emph{same recorded exogenous selection trace}.
This audit does not alter the already executed Gate decision.  Define
\begin{equation}
 D^A_r
 =
 \mathbf 1
 \left\{
   \mathcal A_r^D
   \neq
   \mathcal A_r^0
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:DA_audit_v2}
\end{equation}
When
$\mathcal A_r^D=\mathcal A_r^0\neq\emptyset$, both modes are represented on
common client coordinates and the audit records
\begin{equation}
 \left(
   \Delta_{\beta,r},
   \Delta_{M,r},
   \Delta_{V,r}
 \right).
 \label{eq:common_support_audit_tuple_v2}
\end{equation}
If both counterfactual aggregation sets are empty, the service perturbation is
zero and no $\beta/M/V$ perturbation tuple is formed.
It also logs realized support coverage, target-support retention, ESS
retention, and Design-induced clipping.  These completed-window quantities
may update only the \emph{future} Gate state through certificate models whose
form and update rule were frozen before held-out evaluation.

This post-window construction is central to avoiding a post-hoc Gate.  The
current window first commits to $G_r$ from $\mathcal F_{r^-}$; only after the
window has completed can its realized Full-vs-No-Design discrepancy become
training evidence for future certificates.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Support-Aware Gated Reconstruction (SAG-RAVEN)}
\label{alg:sag_raven}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Frozen target maps, propensity models, certificate models,
thresholds $\boldsymbol\tau^{\mathrm{SAG}}$, look-back horizon, and RAVEN
parameters.
\FOR{window $r=0,1,\ldots$}
  \STATE Update lagged states from completed windows only.
  \STATE Before current $R$ is realized, compute the SAG certificates and
  $\overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}$ from $\mathcal F_{r^-}$.
  \IF{any required certificate is invalid or unavailable}
      \STATE $G_r\gets0$.
  \ELSE
      \STATE Evaluate $G_r$ by \eqref{eq:sag_gate_v2}.
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Freeze $G_r$ for the entire window.
  \STATE Realize the current opportunity process $R_r$.
  \STATE Evaluate and freeze $\widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ from
  $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ before observing $O_{k,r,i}$.
  \STATE Realize the current observation process $O_r$ and form
  $\mathcal E_r$.
  \STATE Set
  $\widetilde\zeta^o_{k,r,i}
  \gets1+G_r(\widehat\zeta^{o,D}_{k,r,i}-1)$.
  \STATE Construct first-stage masses and the pre-$U$ positive-mass set
  $\mathcal B_r$; compute normalized weights, compositions, and
  $n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r}$ on $\mathcal B_r$.
  \STATE Run the local H\'ajek update for clients in $\mathcal B_r$.
  \STATE Form $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ and evaluate/freeze
  $\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ before observing $U_{k,r}$.
  \STATE Realize $U_{k,r}$ and set
  $\mathcal A_r\gets\mathcal B_r\cap\mathcal U_r$.
  \STATE Restrict the pre-$U$ masses/compositions to $\mathcal A_r$, apply
  usable correction, and obtain $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$, $M_r$, $V_r$,
  and $\mathcal C_r$.
  \IF{$\mathcal A_r=\emptyset$}
      \STATE Keep the global model and optional debt state unchanged.
  \ELSE
      \STATE Solve (P2), perform one global update, and update optional Debt.
  \ENDIF
  \STATE After window closure, reconstruct Full-Design and No-Design audit
  statistics on the shared selection trace.
  \STATE Append completed-window audit statistics to the future Gate state only.
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The resulting architecture has a deliberate separation of roles.  SAG decides
\emph{whether} the opportunity/Design correction is support-safe before the
current window; the layered reconstruction determines \emph{what statistical
mass} the usable records represent; and Section~V determines \emph{what
deployment composition} can be realized by the current feasible client set.
No stage uses current or test prediction outcomes to retroactively alter the
same-window Gate.

\section{Feasibility-Aware Deployment-Target Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} determines which statistical mass the usable
records represent after the support-aware opportunity decision and the
subsequent observation/usable-update corrections.  The resulting
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ is a \emph{reference distribution} over the
current aggregation set.  It is not, in general, a feasible final aggregation
decision: mobile availability can make the public target
$\boldsymbol\mu$ unattainable in a finite window, and the corrected reference
itself may violate client-concentration or effective-support constraints.
RAVEN-MCS therefore separates \emph{reconstruction} from
\emph{realization}.  This section maps the reconstructed reference into the
current feasible aggregation set without pretending that unavailable target
mass can be synthesized.

\subsection{From a Corrected Reference to a Feasible Target Image}
\label{subsec:reference_to_feasible}

Consider an active window $r$ with
\begin{equation}
    K_r
    =
    |\mathcal A_r|
    \ge 1.
    \label{eq:Kr_v2}
\end{equation}
Retain the deployment-group composition matrix constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction},
\begin{equation}
    M_r
    =
    [\mathbf c^g_{k,r}]_{k\in\mathcal A_r}
    \in
    \mathbb R^{|\mathcal G|\times K_r}.
    \label{eq:Mr_calibration_v2}
\end{equation}
Each column is a probability vector.  Hence any aggregation distribution
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ over the active clients induces the deployment-group
composition
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\omega_r
    =
    M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r.
    \label{eq:omega_calibration_v2}
\end{equation}

The distinction between
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ and
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ is fundamental.  The former answers

\begin{quote}
\emph{What statistical mass do the currently usable data represent after the
selected support-aware reconstruction?}
\end{quote}

whereas the latter answers

\begin{quote}
\emph{What client mixture can be realized now while remaining close to that
reference and tracking the public deployment target?}
\end{quote}

Accordingly, the reference composition
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\omega_r^{\mathrm{ref}}
    =
    M_r
    \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
    \label{eq:omega_ref_calibration_v2}
\end{equation}
is not assumed to equal $\boldsymbol\mu$ and is not assumed to be the final
service composition.

As introduced in Section~III, the finite-window feasible target image is
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_r
    =
    \left\{
      M_r\boldsymbol\alpha:
      \boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathcal C_r
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:Omega_restate_v2}
\end{equation}
The corresponding irreducible group-level feasibility gap is
\begin{equation}
    d_r^{\mathrm{feas}}
    =
    \min_{\boldsymbol\omega\in\Omega_r}
    \left\|
      \boldsymbol\omega-\boldsymbol\mu
    \right\|_2.
    \label{eq:dfeas_restate_v2}
\end{equation}
Thus every admissible aggregation rule using the same current support obeys
\begin{equation}
    \left\|
      M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r-\boldsymbol\mu
    \right\|_2
    \ge
    d_r^{\mathrm{feas}}.
    \label{eq:dfeas_lower_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
This lower bound makes explicit why statistical target reconstruction and
instantaneous target realization must be treated as different problems.

\subsection{Feasible Aggregation Set and Pre-Aggregation State}
\label{subsec:feasible_set_v2}

The server chooses
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r\in\mathbb R^{K_r}$ from
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_r
 =
 \left\{
 \boldsymbol\alpha:
 \mathbf 1^\top\boldsymbol\alpha=1,\;
 0\le\alpha_k\le\overline\alpha_r,\;
 \|\boldsymbol\alpha\|_2^2
 \le
 \frac{1}{\overline E_r}
 \right\},
 \label{eq:Cr_v2}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \overline\alpha_r
 &=
 \min\!\left\{
 1,
 \max\!\left\{
   \alpha_{\max},
   \frac{1}{K_r}
 \right\}
 \right\},\\
 \overline E_r
 &=
 \min\{E_{\min},K_r\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cap_ess_v2}
\end{equation}
Here $0<\alpha_{\max}\le1$ and $E_{\min}\ge1$ are frozen design
parameters.  The coordinate cap limits single-client dominance, while the
$\ell_2$ constraint enforces the effective aggregation support
\begin{equation}
    E_{\mathrm{agg}}
    \!\left(
      \boldsymbol\alpha_r
    \right)
    \triangleq
    \frac{1}{
      \|\boldsymbol\alpha_r\|_2^2
    }
    \ge
    \overline E_r.
    \label{eq:aggregation_effective_support_v2}
\end{equation}
The uniform vector $\mathbf 1/K_r$ belongs to $\mathcal C_r$; hence every
active window has a nonempty feasible set.  Section~VI uses this property to
establish the existence and uniqueness of the calibration solution.

RAVEN-MCS uses only pre-aggregation uncertainty when assigning client
weights.  Let
$\overline S_{k,r^-}^{\,2}$ be a lagged client-side variance state available
before the current random update is used by the server.  With the
reconstruction-dependent effective sample size
$n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r}$ from Section~IV, define
\begin{equation}
 v_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
   \overline S_{k,r^-}^{\,2}
 }{
   \max\{
     n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r},
     1
   \}
 }
 +
 v_{\mathrm{floor}},
 \qquad
 V_r
 =
 \operatorname{diag}(v_{k,r})
 \succeq0.
 \label{eq:variance_proxy_v2}
\end{equation}
This construction may differ between Full Design and No Design because their
record weights and effective sample sizes may differ; this dependence is
precisely why Section~VI's common-support sensitivity result treats $V_r$ as
a perturbed state rather than as a fixed constant.

Let
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_r
    =
    [\tau_{k,r}]_{k\in\mathcal A_r}
    \succeq\mathbf 0
    \label{eq:staleness_vector_v2}
\end{equation}
be the pre-aggregation staleness vector.  Under the same-window
Full-Design/No-Design counterfactual comparison of Section~IV, this vector is
common whenever the active client coordinates are common because both modes
share the exogenous usable-update trace.

\subsection{Long-Horizon Target Debt as an Accounting State}
\label{subsec:debt_v2}

Instantaneous feasibility may repeatedly under-serve the same deployment
groups.  To record, rather than hide, this unresolved mass, RAVEN-MCS may
maintain a nonnegative target-debt state
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf Q_r
    \in
    \mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal G|},
    \qquad
    \mathbf Q_0=\mathbf 0.
    \label{eq:debt_state_v2}
\end{equation}
For an active window, after
$\boldsymbol\omega_r=M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ has been realized, update
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf Q_{r+1}
 =
 \left[
 \mathbf Q_r
 +
 \eta_r
 \left(
   \boldsymbol\mu
   -
   \boldsymbol\omega_r
 \right)
 \right]_+.
 \label{eq:debt_update_v2}
\end{equation}
If the window is inactive,
$\mathbf Q_{r+1}=\mathbf Q_r$.

A positive $Q_{r,g}$ records accumulated under-service of deployment group
$g$.  Debt does not create unavailable clients, modify the current
support-aware Gate, or retroactively change the reconstructed reference.  It
only provides a lagged inter-window signal to the calibration objective.

For every prefix with the active step mass
\begin{equation}
    S_R
    =
    \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
    \eta_r I_r
    >0,
    \label{eq:SR_debt_v2}
\end{equation}
the recursion implies the deterministic accounting relation
\begin{equation}
 \left\|
   \overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R
   -
   \boldsymbol\mu
 \right\|_1
 \le
 \frac{
   2
   \|\mathbf Q_R\|_1
 }{
   S_R
 }.
 \label{eq:debt_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
The proof is deferred to the Appendix.  Equation~\eqref{eq:debt_bound_v2}
is an accounting statement: sublinear debt implies vanishing prefix
structural mismatch, but the paper does not require or claim that Debt has a
large isolated empirical effect.

For applications that do not use long-horizon accounting, set
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf Q_r\equiv\mathbf 0.
    \label{eq:no_debt_mode_v2}
\end{equation}
The support-aware reconstruction and instantaneous calibration remain
well-defined.  This makes Debt an optional long-horizon extension rather than
a separate innovation.

\subsection{Feasibility-Aware Calibration Program}
\label{subsec:P2_v2}

For every active window, RAVEN-MCS solves
\begin{equation}
 \text{(P2)}
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star
 \in
 \arg\min_{\boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathcal C_r}
 J_r(\boldsymbol\alpha).
 \label{eq:P2_v2}
\end{equation}
The objective is
\begin{align}
 J_r(\boldsymbol\alpha)
 ={}&
 -\mathbf Q_r^\top
 M_r\boldsymbol\alpha
 +
 \frac{\lambda_g}{2}
 \left\|
   M_r\boldsymbol\alpha
   -
   \boldsymbol\mu
 \right\|_2^2
 \nonumber\\
 &+
 \frac{\lambda_\beta}{2}
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\alpha
   -
   \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
 \right\|_2^2
 \nonumber\\
 &+
 \frac{\lambda_v}{2}
 \boldsymbol\alpha^\top
 V_r
 \boldsymbol\alpha
 +
 \lambda_s
 \overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_r^\top
 \boldsymbol\alpha.
 \label{eq:P2_objective_v2}
\end{align}
The frozen coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_\beta>0,
    \qquad
    \lambda_g,\lambda_v,\lambda_s\ge0.
    \label{eq:P2_lambdas_v2}
\end{equation}

The terms have distinct but coordinated roles.  The first term favors groups
with accumulated under-service when Debt is enabled.  The second term tracks
the public deployment target inside the current feasible image
$\Omega_r$.  The third anchors the solution to the selection-corrected
reference produced by Section~IV.  The fourth discourages concentration on
clients with larger pre-aggregation uncertainty, and the last term penalizes
stale usable updates.  These terms constitute one feasibility-aware
calibration program; they are not presented as independent algorithmic
contributions.

The reference term is especially important under SAG.  When the Gate is
OFF, Theorem~2 will show that the ideal corrected measure moves toward the
opportunity law rather than pretending to equal the public target.
P2 does not erase that statistical meaning.  Instead, it determines how far
the currently feasible aggregation can move toward $\boldsymbol\mu$ without
abandoning the reference, uncertainty, staleness, and client-support
constraints.  Conversely, when the Gate is ON, P2 cannot create target mass
outside $\Omega_r$.  In both cases, reconstruction and calibration retain
separate semantics:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \text{selection representation}
 &\longrightarrow
 \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r,\\
 \text{feasible realization}
 &\longrightarrow
 \boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:reconstruction_realization_chain_v2}
\end{equation}

The parameters in
\eqref{eq:Cr_v2}--\eqref{eq:P2_lambdas_v2} are selected only from the
development protocol and are frozen before genuinely unseen evaluation.
The online SAG decision does not tune or modify them using current/test
prediction outcomes.

\subsection{Global Update and Window-Close Logic}
\label{subsec:global_update_v2}

Let $\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star$ be the unique P2 solution established in
Section~VI.  The server performs
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\theta_{r+1}
 =
 \boldsymbol\theta_r
 -
 \eta_r
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal A_r}
 \alpha_{k,r}^\star
 \mathbf u_{k,r}.
 \label{eq:server_update_v2}
\end{equation}
The realized deployment-group composition is
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\omega_r
    =
    M_r
    \boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star.
    \label{eq:omega_star_v2}
\end{equation}
If Debt is enabled, the server then applies
\eqref{eq:debt_update_v2}.  Only after the current aggregation/debt step is
complete are the lagged states advanced for future windows.

If
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal A_r=\varnothing,
    \label{eq:inactive_condition_v2}
\end{equation}
the window is inactive:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\theta_{r+1}
    =
    \boldsymbol\theta_r,
    \qquad
    \mathbf Q_{r+1}
    =
    \mathbf Q_r,
    \label{eq:inactive_update_v2}
\end{equation}
and the window contributes no active step mass to $S_R$.

Together with Algorithm~\ref{alg:sag_raven}, the complete window order is
therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 &\rightarrow G_r
 \rightarrow R
 \rightarrow \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}\ \text{freeze}
 \rightarrow O
 \rightarrow \mathcal B_r\\
 &\rightarrow \text{local update}
 \rightarrow \widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}\ \text{freeze}
 \rightarrow U
 \rightarrow \mathcal A_r\\
 &\rightarrow \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
 \rightarrow \text{(P2)}
 \rightarrow \boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star
 \rightarrow \text{global/debt update}\\
 &\rightarrow \text{post-window Full/No-Design audit}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:raven_pipeline_v2}
\end{equation}
The ordering is deliberate.  SAG controls whether Full Design is
support-safe before the current selection process; the reconstruction layer
determines what the selected usable data represent; P2 maps that reference
onto the current feasible target image; and only completed-window structural
audits may affect future Gate certificates.

\subsection{Interface to the Safety Analysis}
\label{subsec:calibration_safety_interface}

The same P2 program is used under Full Design and the No-Design fallback.
For a one-window counterfactual comparison starting from the same
pre-window state, the two modes may nevertheless produce different
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r,
    \qquad
    M_r,
    \qquad
    V_r,
    \label{eq:P2_perturbed_states_v2}
\end{equation}
and may even produce different active client sets.  Therefore the
beta-only non-expansion result used in the earlier RAVEN analysis is not
sufficient for SAG.

Section~VI addresses these two cases separately.  On a common active set, it
uses the $\lambda_\beta$-strong convexity of P2 to bound the change in
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star$ jointly in
$\Delta_{\beta,r}$,
$\Delta_{M,r}$, and
$\Delta_{V,r}$.  When the active sets differ, it combines that
common-support sensitivity with the predictable mismatch-risk certificate
$\overline\delta_{A,r}$ and the explicit certificate-failure probability
$\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}$.  The resulting bound is exactly the
$\overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}$ quantity already used by SAG in
Section~IV.

This interface also fixes the claim boundary.  The calibration theory
controls service-composition perturbation and per-window structural harm
relative to the No-Design fallback.  It does not, under the present
assumptions, imply a universal bound on deployment-target RMSE or
WorstGroupRMSE because the reconstruction weights can also change the local
learning trajectory.  Prediction no-harm is therefore evaluated empirically
under the frozen protocol rather than asserted by P2.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We analyze the three questions induced by Sections~III--V: whether the
deployment and arrival laws define the same learning objective, what
statistical law is represented by predictable gated correction, and how much
Full Design can perturb the feasible calibration relative to the No-Design
RAVEN fallback.  The analysis deliberately separates ideal identification
from finite-window stabilization.  It does not claim recovery outside
identifiable support, robustness to arbitrary hidden confounding, exact
unbiasedness under active clipping, universal prediction-risk improvement, or
trajectory-level equivalence between counterfactual algorithms.

\subsection{Assumptions and Scope}
\label{subsec:assumptions_v2}

\noindent\textbf{A1 (Compatible target support and positivity).}
The public target measures in Section~III satisfy
\eqref{eq:atomic_target_v2}--\eqref{eq:target_compatibility_v2}.
Whenever $\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}>0$ is eligible for Full-Design
reconstruction,
\begin{equation}
    P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)>0,
    \qquad
    p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}>0,
    \qquad
    q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}>0
    \label{eq:A1_v2}
\end{equation}
on the corresponding sequential events.

\noindent\textbf{A2 (Sequential pre-outcome ignorability).}
Conditional on the legal information available at each stage, observation and
usable-update selection are conditionally mean-ignorable for the bounded
moment being reconstructed.  In particular, for every relevant registered
attempt,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \frac{U_{k,r}}{q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}}
 \right|
 \mathcal H^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r},
 O_{k,r,i}=1,
 R_{k,r,i}=1
 \right]
 =1,
 \label{eq:A2_use_v2}
\end{equation}
and the analogous observation identity holds under
$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$.  Neither history contains the random
outcome it is used to correct.

\noindent\textbf{A3 (Correct ideal ratios and cluster-level regularity).}
For exact identification, the ideal opportunity, observation, and
usable-update probabilities equal their population counterparts and the
inverse factors are untruncated, or the stabilizing bounds are inactive on the
relevant support.  Along a growing sequence of opportunity-event
clusters/windows, the weighted numerator and denominator obey a weak law of
large numbers, and the normalized denominator converges in probability to a
strictly positive limit.

\noindent\textbf{A4 (Convex calibration).}
In every active window,
$K_r\ge1$, $0<\alpha_{\max}\le1$, $E_{\min}\ge1$,
$V_r\succeq0$, $\lambda_\beta>0$, and
$\lambda_g,\lambda_v,\lambda_s\ge0$.  The feasible set is
\eqref{eq:Cr_v2}--\eqref{eq:cap_ess_v2}.

\noindent\textbf{A5 (Quantitative fixed-support plug-in regularity).}
On the target-supported events of A1, the opportunity, observation, and
usable-update probabilities are bounded away from zero by positive constants.
The plug-in errors satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \sup |\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}-\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}|
 &\le \epsilon_\pi,\\
 \sup |\widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}-p^{\mathrm{obs}}|
 &\le \epsilon_p,\\
 \sup |\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}-q^{\mathrm{use}}|
 &\le \epsilon_q,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:A5_plugin_errors_v2}
\end{equation}
and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}$ bounds the aggregate residual introduced by
truncating the corresponding unclipped observation/Design and usable-update
ratios.  The ideal and implemented H\'ajek normalizing denominators are
bounded below by $B_{\min}>0$, and the same-window ideal and practical
constructions induce the same $\mathcal A_r$, $M_r$, and $\mathcal C_r$.
This assumption is intentionally local: it fails when Design changes support
or when target-zero opportunity mass changes the active client set.

\noindent\textbf{A6 (Gate predictability).}
The operational Gate $G_r$ and all quantities used to compute it are
$\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable.  Hence $G_r$ is frozen before the current
$R/O/U$ realization and cannot depend on current sensing values, prediction
residuals, update coordinates, future events, or test outcomes.

\noindent\textbf{A7 (Shared exogenous selection trace).}
Conditional on the common pre-window state and legal stage histories, the
Full-Design and No-Design counterfactuals use the same realized
$R/O/\mathcal E_r/U$ trace.  Design changes statistical weights and the local
objective, but does not change the exogenous opportunity, observation,
registration, computation, network, deadline, or staleness events in this
comparison.

\noindent\textbf{A8a (Active-set-risk upper validity).}
The certificate $\overline\delta_{A,r}$ is
$\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable and, on the frozen Gate applicability region,
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{A,r}
 =
 \Pr\!\left(
   \mathcal A_r^D\neq\mathcal A_r^0
   \mid
   \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right)
 \le
 \overline\delta_{A,r}.
 \label{eq:A8a_v2}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{A8b (Common-nonempty-support perturbation coverage).}
Partition active-set equality into
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_r^+
 &=
 \{\mathcal A_r^D=\mathcal A_r^0\neq\emptyset\},
 \label{eq:common_active_event_v2}\\
 \mathcal E_r^{\emptyset}
 &=
 \{\mathcal A_r^D=\mathcal A_r^0=\emptyset\}.
 \label{eq:both_empty_event_v2}
\end{align}
The $\beta/M/V$ perturbations are defined only on $\mathcal E_r^+$.  Whenever
$\Pr(\mathcal E_r^+\mid\mathcal F_{r^-})>0$, conditional on
$\mathcal F_{r^-}$ and $\mathcal E_r^+$ the pre-window upper certificates
satisfy; if this conditional event has zero probability, the A8b requirement
is vacuous for that window
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\!\bigl(
 &\Delta_{\beta,r}
 \le
 \overline\Delta_{\beta,r},\;
 \Delta_{M,r}
 \le
 \overline\Delta_{M,r},\\[-1mm]
 &\Delta_{V,r}
 \le
 \overline\Delta_{V,r}
 \mid
 \mathcal F_{r^-},
 \mathcal E_r^+
 \bigr)
 \ge
 1-\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:A8b_v2}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}$ is frozen before unseen evaluation.

\noindent\textbf{A9 (Gated-support sequential identification).}
For any predictable $g_r\in[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{gate}}
 =
 \left\{
 (k,i):
 g_r\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
 +
 (1-g_r)P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)
 >
 0
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:Sgate_v2}
\end{equation}
On $\mathcal S_r^{\mathrm{gate}}$, the opportunity ratio is well-defined,
$p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ and $q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ are positive on the
events to which their inverse factors are applied, and the sequential
mean-ignorability identities of A2 hold.  When $g_r=1$, A9 reduces to the
Full-Design target-supported identification region; when $g_r=0$, it also
covers opportunity-positive but target-zero marks retained by the No-Design
fallback.

\subsection{Target Risk Is Not Arrival Risk}
\label{subsec:prop_risk_v2}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (Target--arrival risk distinction).}
Let
$\boldsymbol\varpi^{\mathrm{tar}}$ and
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathrm{arr}}$ be normalized laws on the same
finite atomic domain.  If
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\varpi^{\mathrm{tar}}
    \neq
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathrm{arr}},
    \label{eq:unequal_laws_v2}
\end{equation}
then there exists a bounded loss profile
$\mathbf L=(L_i)_i$ for which
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i
 \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}L_i
 \neq
 \sum_i
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}L_i.
 \label{eq:risk_noninterchange_v2}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, equality of the two risk functionals for every bounded loss
profile requires equality of the underlying laws.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

Proposition~1 does not claim that every trained predictor must exhibit a large
numerical risk gap.  It establishes that the arrival law cannot be substituted
for the deployment law by convention.

\subsection{Full-Design Target Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:prop_reconstruction_v2}

For an opportunity event $e\in\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$ in window $r$ with
mark $X_{r,e}=(k_e,i_e)$ as defined in Section~III, define the ideal
Full-Design contribution.  By convention, a contribution is zero unless the
event is observed and its client forms a registered attempt; the
$q^{\mathrm{use}}$ factor is evaluated only on such registered attempts
\begin{equation}
 \chi_e
 =
 O_eU_{k_e,r}
 \frac{
   \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k_e,i_e}
 }{
   P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k_e,i_e)
   p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k_e,r,i_e}
   q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k_e,r}
 }.
 \label{eq:chi_full_design_v2}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2 (Normalized Full-Design target reconstruction).}
Under A1--A3, for any bounded function $\phi(k,i)$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Phi_R^D
 =
 \frac{
   \sum_{r<R}
   \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}}
   \chi_e
   \phi(k_e,i_e)
 }{
   \sum_{r<R}
   \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}}
   \chi_e
 }
 \xrightarrow{p}
 \Phi^{\mathrm{tar}},
 \label{eq:prop2_full_design_v2}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    \Phi^{\mathrm{tar}}
    =
    \sum_{k,i}
    \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
    \phi(k,i).
    \label{eq:phi_target_v2}
\end{equation}
Consequently, client--stratum indicators recover
$\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}$, and
$\phi(k,i)=\mathbf1\{h(i)=g\}$ recovers the deployment-group mass $\mu_g$.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

This is an ideal pooled consistency statement.  It does not imply that the
finite-window clipped reference
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ exactly realizes
$\boldsymbol\mu$.

\subsection{Existence and Uniqueness of Feasible Calibration}
\label{subsec:theorem_calibration_v2}

\noindent\textbf{Theorem 1 (Unique feasible calibration).}
Under A4, every active window has a nonempty compact convex feasible set
$\mathcal C_r$, and program~\eqref{eq:P2_v2} has a unique global minimizer
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star.
    \label{eq:alpha_unique_v2}
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

The theorem concerns the mathematical program.  It does not retrospectively
certify exact optimality of every numerical solver output.

\subsection{Fixed-Support Plug-In Stability and Its Limitation}
\label{subsec:plugin_scope_v2}

The previous RAVEN analysis also provides a local finite-window plug-in result.
Let $\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star$ be the same-window ideal untruncated reference
and $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ the implemented clipped plug-in.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3 (Practical plug-in perturbation on fixed support).}
Under A1, A4, and A5, there exists the explicit finite constant $C_1$ given
in Appendix~A such that
\begin{equation}
 \left\|
   \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
   -
   \boldsymbol\beta_r^\star
 \right\|_1
 \le
 C_1
 \left(
   \epsilon_\pi
   +
   \epsilon_p
   +
   \epsilon_q
   +
   \epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:plugin_beta_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
At the same fixed window state, with $M_r$, $V_r$, $\mathcal C_r$, and the
active coordinates held fixed,
\begin{align}
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\alpha^\star(
     \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
   )
   -
   \boldsymbol\alpha^\star(
     \boldsymbol\beta_r^\star
   )
 \right\|_2
 &\le
 \left\|
   \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
   -
   \boldsymbol\beta_r^\star
 \right\|_2,
 \label{eq:plugin_alpha_nonexpand_v2}\\
 \left\|
 M_r
 \left[
   \boldsymbol\alpha^\star(
     \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
   )
   -
   \boldsymbol\alpha^\star(
     \boldsymbol\beta_r^\star
   )
 \right]
 \right\|_2
 &\le
 \|M_r\|_2
 \left\|
   \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r
   -
   \boldsymbol\beta_r^\star
 \right\|_2.
 \label{eq:plugin_service_nonexpand_v2}
\end{align}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

Proposition~3 is retained only as a fixed-support stabilization result.  It
does not compare Full Design with No Design when $M_r$, $V_r$, or
$\mathcal A_r$ changes.  The next two theorems address precisely that missing
regime.

\subsection{Predictable Gated Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:thm_gated_law_v2}

\noindent\textbf{Theorem 2 (Predictable gated reconstruction law).}
Fix window $r$ and let $g_r\in[0,1]$ be
$\mathcal F_{r^-}$-measurable.  For every opportunity-event mark with
$P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \zeta^{o,(g)}_{k,r,i}
 =
 1
 +
 g_r
 \left[
   \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}}{P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)}
   -1
 \right].
 \label{eq:gated_ratio_theory_v2}
\end{equation}
Assume A6 and A9 and the correct, untruncated ideal stage probabilities in the
identification part of A3.  As above, the usable-update inverse factor is
evaluated only for registered attempts, with zero contribution otherwise.
For a generic opportunity event
$e\in\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$ with mark $X_{r,e}$, define the conditional
ideal corrected event measure
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_r^{(g)}(k,i)
 =
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \mathbf 1\{X_{r,e}=(k,i)\}
 O_e U_{k_e,r}
 \frac{\zeta^{o,(g)}_{k_e,r,i_e}}
 {p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k_e,r,i_e}q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k_e,r}}
 \right|
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Gamma_gated_v2}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_r^{(g)}(k,i)
 =
 g_r\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
 +(1-g_r)P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i).
 }
 \label{eq:gated_mixture_law_v2}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
$\sum_{k,i}\Gamma_r^{(g)}(k,i)=1$, and for any bounded $\phi$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_r^{(g)}
 =
 g_r\Phi^{\mathrm{tar}}
 +(1-g_r)\Phi_r^{\mathrm{opp}}.
 \label{eq:gated_moment_v2}
\end{equation}
For the deployment-group indicator this gives
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\mu_r^{(g)}
 =
 g_r\boldsymbol\mu
 +(1-g_r)\boldsymbol\mu_r^{\mathrm{opp}},
 \label{eq:gated_group_mixture_v2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\mu_r^{(g)}-\boldsymbol\mu
 \right\|_1
 =
 (1-g_r)
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\mu_r^{\mathrm{opp}}-\boldsymbol\mu
 \right\|_1.
 }
 \label{eq:gated_group_distance_v2}
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

Theorem~2 is an exact conditional \emph{opportunity-event corrected-measure}
identity.  It does not equate
$P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)$ with
$\mathbb E[R_{k,r,i}\mid\mathcal F_{r^-}]$ and does not assert an exact
finite-sample H\'ajek equality.  Setting $g_r=1$ recovers the ideal
Full-Design target law; setting $g_r=0$ yields the ideal O/U-corrected
opportunity law.  If the cluster/window regularity part of A3 also holds, the
pooled normalized estimator converges to the corresponding event/window-
weighted average of these gated laws.

\subsection{Common-Support Calibration Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:thm_common_support_v2}

\noindent\textbf{Theorem 3 (Common-support calibration sensitivity).}
Fix one window and compare Full Design $D$ with No Design $0$ from the same
pre-window state.  Suppose both modes have the same nonempty active set,
placed in the same canonical client order, and therefore the same feasible
set $\mathcal C_r$.  The two P2 problems share
$\mathbf Q_r$, $\boldsymbol\mu$, $\overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_r$,
$\lambda_g$, $\lambda_\beta$, $\lambda_v$, $\lambda_s$, and
$\mathcal C_r$; only $\boldsymbol\beta_r$, $M_r$, and $V_r$ are perturbed.
Let
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\beta,r}
 &=
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\beta_r^D
   -
   \boldsymbol\beta_r^0
 \right\|_2,
 \nonumber\\
 \Delta_{M,r}
 &=
 \left\|
   M_r^D
   -
   M_r^0
 \right\|_2,
 \nonumber\\
 \Delta_{V,r}
 &=
 \left\|
   V_r^D
   -
   V_r^0
 \right\|_2,
 \label{eq:three_perturbations_v2}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{cf}}
 =
 \max\{
   \|M_r^D\|_2,
   \|M_r^0\|_2
 \},
 \qquad
 B_{Q,r}
 =
 \|\mathbf Q_r\|_2.
 \label{eq:BM_BQ_v2}
\end{equation}
Let
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r^D$ and
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r^0$ be the corresponding P2 minimizers.  Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^D
   -
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^0
 \right\|_2
 \le{}&
 \Delta_{\beta,r}\\
 &+
 \frac{
   B_{Q,r}
   +
   \lambda_g
   (2B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{cf}}+1)
 }{
   \lambda_\beta
 }
 \Delta_{M,r}\\
 &+
 \frac{\lambda_v}{\lambda_\beta}
 \Delta_{V,r}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:T3_alpha_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \left\|
   M_r^D
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^D
   -
   M_r^0
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^0
 \right\|_2
 \le
 \Delta_{M,r}
 +
 B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{cf}}
 \left\|
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^D
   -
   \boldsymbol\alpha_r^0
 \right\|_2.
}
\label{eq:T3_service_bound_v2}
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

The proof uses the
$\lambda_\beta$-strong convexity of P2 and the variational inequalities of
the two constrained minimizers.  Unlike
Proposition~3, Theorem~3 jointly accounts for changes in the reconstructed
reference, the client composition matrix, and the variance state.  It is a
same-window, same-pre-window-state sensitivity result; it is not a
trajectory-level comparison of two algorithms run from time zero.

\subsection{Active-Set-Risk Structural Safety}
\label{subsec:thm_active_set_v2}

For mode $m\in\{D,0\}$, define the service vector
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf s_r^m
 =
 I_r^m
 M_r^m
 \boldsymbol\alpha_r^m,
 \label{eq:service_vector_mode_v2}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf s_r^m=\mathbf0$ for an inactive mode.  On the nonempty
common-active-set event, Theorem~3 and A8b provide the certified
$\ell_1$ bound
\begin{equation}
 \overline B_r^{\mathrm{srv}}
 =
 \sqrt{|\mathcal G|}
 \left(
   \overline\Delta_{M,r}
   +
   B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}
   \overline D_r^\alpha
 \right),
 \label{eq:Bsrv_theory_v2}
\end{equation}
where $B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the predictable Gate-side upper bound
satisfying
\begin{equation}
 B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}
 \ge
 B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{cf}},
 \qquad
 B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}=\sqrt K
 \ \text{is always valid},
 \label{eq:BM_pre_cf_relation_v2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \overline D_r^\alpha
 =
 &\overline\Delta_{\beta,r}
 +
 \frac{
   B_{Q,r}
   +
   \lambda_g
   (2B_{M,r}^{\mathrm{pre}}+1)
 }{
   \lambda_\beta
 }
 \overline\Delta_{M,r}\\
 &+
 \frac{\lambda_v}{\lambda_\beta}
 \overline\Delta_{V,r}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Dalpha_theory_v2}
\end{equation}
If both counterfactual active sets are empty, their service difference is zero.

\noindent\textbf{Theorem 4 (Active-set-risk structural perturbation bound).}
Under A4 and A6--A8, condition on $\mathcal F_{r^-}$ and compare Full Design
with the No-Design fallback on the A7 shared selection trace.  Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \left\|
   \mathbf s_r^D
   -
   \mathbf s_r^0
 \right\|_1
 \right|
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right]
 \le{}&
 \overline B_r^{\mathrm{srv}}
 +
 2\overline\delta_{A,r}\\
 &+
 2\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}
 =
 \overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:T4_total_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
For the binary SAG action
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf s_r^{\mathrm{SAG}}
 =
 G_r\mathbf s_r^D
 +
 (1-G_r)\mathbf s_r^0,
 \label{eq:sag_service_vector_v2}
\end{equation}
the Gate rule
$\overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}\le\tau_{\mathrm{safe}}$ whenever $G_r=1$
implies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \left\|
   \mathbf s_r^{\mathrm{SAG}}
   -
   \mathbf s_r^0
 \right\|_1
 \right|
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right]
 \le
 \tau_{\mathrm{safe}}.
 }
 \label{eq:T4_sag_bound_v2}
\end{equation}
If $G_r=0$, the SAG-vs-fallback service perturbation is exactly zero.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See Appendix~A.

The proof partitions the window into four cases:
$\mathcal E_r^+$ with a valid common-support certificate,
$\mathcal E_r^+$ with certificate failure,
$\mathcal E_r^{\emptyset}$, and active-set mismatch.  The both-empty case has
zero service difference.  The mismatch event contributes at most the
$\ell_1$ diameter $2$ of the zero-extended deployment-group simplex, while
A8b contributes the explicit common-nonempty certificate-failure term
$2\delta_{\mathrm{cal},r}$.  This is why both terms appear in
\eqref{eq:T4_total_bound_v2}.

\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1 (Per-window structural-harm bound).}
Define
\begin{equation}
    L_r^m
    =
    \left\|
      \mathbf s_r^m
      -
      \boldsymbol\mu
    \right\|_1.
    \label{eq:per_window_target_discrepancy_v2}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \bigl(
   L_r^D-L_r^0
 \bigr)_+
 \right|
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right]
 \le
 \overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}.
 }
 \label{eq:cor_structural_harm_full_v2}
\end{equation}
For the actual SAG action,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left.
 \bigl(
   L_r^{\mathrm{SAG}}-L_r^0
 \bigr)_+
 \right|
 \mathcal F_{r^-}
 \right]
 \le
 G_r
 \overline B_r^{\mathrm{tot}}
 \le
 \tau_{\mathrm{safe}}
 }
 \label{eq:cor_structural_harm_sag_v2}
\end{equation}
whenever the Gate is ON.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
By the reverse triangle inequality,
$(L_r^D-L_r^0)_+
\le
\|\mathbf s_r^D-\mathbf s_r^0\|_1$; apply Theorem~4.
The SAG statement follows from the predictability of $G_r$.
\hfill$\square$

Corollary~1 gives the precise meaning of \emph{structural safety}: the Gate
controls expected \emph{per-window} excess deployment-target discrepancy
relative to the No-Design RAVEN fallback.  It is not a direct theorem for the
prefix metric $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}(R)$, whose normalization can differ
when the two modes induce different active-window sets.

\subsection{Implications and Claim Boundary}
\label{subsec:theory_implications_v2}

The results form a single support-aware chain.  Proposition~1 establishes why
the arrival population cannot replace the deployment population by convention.
Proposition~2 gives the classical Full-Design target-law interpretation under
supported sequential identification.  Theorem~2 then shows what changes when
the opportunity correction is predictably gated: the exact conditional
corrected measure interpolates between the deployment law and the opportunity
law rather than silently changing its statistical meaning.  Theorem~3
quantifies how Full Design can perturb feasible calibration even when the
active client coordinates are unchanged, and Theorem~4 extends the analysis
to uncertain active-set changes and imperfect certificates.  P2 remains the
feasibility map connecting reconstructed statistical mass to what can
actually be realized in a finite mobile window.

The guarantees stop at this structural boundary.  SAG changes local record
weights and can therefore change the local objective and the subsequent model
trajectory.  Without additional learner-stability assumptions, none of
Theorems~2--4 implies universal no-harm for
$\mathcal R_\mu$, RMSE, or WorstGroupRMSE.  Prediction no-harm and
prefix-level structural performance are therefore evaluated under the frozen
experimental protocol rather than asserted as theoretical consequences.
